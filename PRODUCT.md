# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Firefly serves stray animal rescue volunteers, neighborhood feeders, local pet owners, community informants, rescue station operators, and friendly clinics. They often use the product outdoors, on mobile, under time pressure, with incomplete information and a need to protect sensitive locations and contact details.

## Product Purpose

Firefly is a map-first community mutual-aid tool for reporting, discovering, and coordinating stray animal risk alerts and rescue resources. Success means users can quickly understand what is happening nearby, submit useful reports with confidence, and find a low-friction way to help without exposing private information.

## Brand Personality

Calm, helpful, credible. The interface should feel like a trusted neighborhood coordination tool: warm enough to invite participation, disciplined enough to handle risk, and direct enough for mobile field use.

## Anti-references

Do not make Firefly feel like a generic social app, donation campaign, marketing landing page, emergency siren dashboard, or decorative map poster. Avoid oversized hero sections, purple or blue gradients, emoji-led controls, glassmorphism, high-saturation red surfaces, large rounded cards, and template-like feature grids.

## Design Principles

1. Map first, action close: the first screen should show nearby context and place the next useful action within thumb reach.
2. Collaboration over alarm: risk needs urgency, but the dominant feeling should be that neighbors can help each other.
3. Privacy builds trust: approximate locations, contact protection, and clear visibility language are part of the core experience.
4. Two tracks, one system: risk alerts and aid resources must be visually distinct while still belonging to the same product language.
5. Multilingual by default: Chinese, English, and Hindi layouts must be treated as primary, not translations squeezed into a Chinese-only design.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for all non-map UI. Controls must support 44px minimum touch targets, keyboard focus, reduced motion, and color-independent category recognition through icons and text. Key map information must also be available in list or sheet form for users who cannot rely on visual map markers alone.
